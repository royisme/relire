# Changelog

What changed in Relire and why, newest first. Format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com); the project is not released yet, so everything sits under **Unreleased** until the first tagged version.

**How to use this file:** every change a user could notice (a feature, a behaviour change, a removal, a fix) gets a line under Unreleased in the same commit that makes it. Group by *Added / Changed / Fixed / Removed*. Say what the user gets, not which files moved. When a version is tagged, rename Unreleased to the version and date and start a new Unreleased.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Four more compound tenses.** Plus-que-parfait, futur antérieur, conditionnel passé and subjonctif passé appear on the Conjugation page, the word sheet, in drills and in sentence patterns. Verbs in the grammar pack are reviewed; AI-made tables are not checked.
- **Noun gender without a key.** My study → Grammar → Gender shows all 17 reviewed ending tendencies with dictionary and A1 counts, examples and sample exceptions. Make local drills from 337 reviewed A1 nouns, including exceptions; checking reveals the saved tendency and exception note. Noun lookups show the longest matching ending under the meaning, without asserting an unknown noun's gender.
- The supplied vocabulary source has a private normalized draft and recorded attribution/hash metadata. It is not marked reviewed or available for download; content approval is still required before local publication.
- Words now has Today, paginated All words and reveal-first review routes. Personal contexts and built-in snapshots share canonical cards, minute learning steps, daily introduction limits and durable rating retries; paired backups preserve quota history and Grammar metadata. A private normalization/review/staged-publication pipeline and complete-book offline loader are implemented with synthetic tests only. Real corpus review, C1/Canadian-usage decisions, D1 application and public distribution remain owner gates.
- The first-chapter Grammar learning pilot supports short teaching slices, six choice and typed questions per topic, accent-sensitive checks and bilingual feedback. Revisit saved answers in Practice, review feedback in Mistakes, or redo/reset without losing history. The private authoring/review pipeline now preserves the complete lesson; code is verified with synthetic data only, and real twelve-question review/local acceptance remain pending.
- First-chapter noun gender and plural topics are included for local content-service testing after owner-authorized AI review, not human language review.
- **Common verbs show their tenses without a key.** When a content service is configured, the word sheet's conjugation slot shows a common verb's simple tenses from the Morphalou dictionary (marked as not reviewed; compound tenses stay on the Conjugation page), and tapping any conjugated form of such a verb adds a "Verb form" line naming the verb, tense and person. The About page lists the data source, its licence and how to rebuild it.
- Grammar Topics are available only when a content service is configured, with offline copies of fetched content; without a service, Conjugation and Gender remain available.
- Grammar tags in mistakes, practice and Progress link to their topics when one is published.
- **Topics in My study → Grammar.** The tab now opens Topics, with an honest empty state because no topics are published yet; Conjugation remains a separate section.
- **Hear conjugations without a key.** Listen beside each form, participle or example on the Conjugation page and each form in the word sheet, using this device’s French voice; playback and errors stay on the fixed-size control.
- **See how conjugations are built.** Regular forms underline the engine’s endings, and the Conjugation page shows the rule’s ending table from the same generator; irregular forms stay unsplit.
- **Sentence patterns on the Conjugation page.** Choose a tense and person to see negation, an est-ce que question and an inversion question, without another AI call; unavailable patterns say why and unchecked forms keep their note. The pattern rules await language review.
- **Conjugation drills without a key.** Practice → Add exercises has a third choice: pick verbs from the reviewed conjugation pack and the tenses to practise, and each tense becomes its own exercise (write the verb in the form asked). No AI is called, so it works with no key configured; accents always count on these items, whatever the strict-accents preference says, and a missing accent is marked wrong with the right form beside it.
- **The word sheet shows real conjugations.** A verb in the reviewed list shows its table from that list, with no AI; any other verb shows a "Conjugate this verb" link to the Conjugation page with the verb filled in; tapping a conjugated form (`vais`) adds a "Verb form" line naming the verb, tense and person. The AI no longer writes a table for a looked-up word, and the Conjugation page has a "Practise this verb" button that makes a drill set for the verb on screen.
- **Conjugation page under My study → Grammar.** Type a French verb in the infinitive to see its tenses, each with when it is used and how it is formed, the auxiliary, the participles and, for a verb the AI answered, an example sentence marked AI-made. The few verbs in the app's reviewed list work with no key and no AI call; any other verb needs your text AI key and is compared with the app's own rules, so it is shown as matching the rule, partly differing (with the model's and the rule's form side by side) or AI-made and not checked, never as verified. A failure, such as a missing key, shows its real reason next to the input. `#/study/grammar/conjugation?verb=parler` opens it with a verb already run.
- **Importing exercises opens the new exercise set.** After a successful import the new exercise set page opens, listing its exercises, instead of the first exercise.
- **Delete a whole batch of exercises.** Each batch on Practice has a "Delete this batch" button that asks first, then removes the batch's units and the answers on them in one step; your history of attempts stays in Mistakes and Progress. If the delete fails, the dialog stays open and shows the reason. Batches are now listed newest first, because every batch remembers when it was made.
- **Each speech engine keeps its own model and voice.** Switch Gemini TTS → Fish Audio → Gemini and each comes back with the model and voice you last chose for it, instead of resetting to defaults; the choices are validated on save like any other setting, so a value the provider would refuse is never stored silently.
- **Automatic playback asks before it costs anything.** Settings → Practice has a new opt-in, off by default: until you turn it on, the automatic replay of a dictation item after its check uses only this device's voice, and nothing is synthesized remotely for that automatic replay. Pressing Listen on the item, or Preview in Settings, still uses the engine chosen in Speech synthesis as always. The line under it says what the provider may charge.
- **Speak with this device's own voice.** Speech synthesis now offers the browser's French voice as an engine beside Gemini TTS and Fish Audio: it needs no key, costs nothing and stores no audio, so it also works offline. Choosing it hides the key, model and voice fields and says in one line what it does and what it depends on; Preview voice still plays the test phrase and reports when the device has no French voice. It is a choice, not a fallback: a remote engine that fails still reports its own error.
- **Hear any exercise prompt.** Every item of the sheet except a dictation now has a Listen control beside its grammar topic: it speaks the item's own audio when it has some, otherwise the prompt with its blanks and `{a|b}` choices left unsaid, so no markup is read aloud. Clips go through the same speech layer as the rest of the app (made once, then kept and counted in Settings), only one sound plays at a time, and a dictation item keeps its single Listen beside the field.
- **Redo one item without losing the rest of the sheet.** Every item carries a ghost Redo from the start, off until the item is checked: it then clears that item's answer and result so you can try it again, while every other answer on the sheet stays. Because the button is always there, checking and redoing leave the rows below where they were. Your attempts are history, so the mistake stays in the notebook and a later correct attempt resolves it; the setting is silent about deleting anything because nothing is deleted.
- **Counts where you are answering.** The sheet's header now shows "n of N checked, m right" above the items, from the same progress the Practice list reads, instead of only below the last row.
- **Mistake count in the sidebar.** The Mistakes row under My study carries a badge with the number of mistakes no later attempt has resolved, kept live as you practise; it never counts resolved ones.
- **Use Ollama to run everything on your own machine.** Pick Ollama for text answers or image reading and it talks to the server you run (default `http://localhost:11434`, changeable in the Base URL field): no key, no cost, and nothing leaving your device. The model list is fetched from that server and any model name can be typed and is sent as typed, so an unconfirmed model answers with its own error, and the key field says the local server must allow this page's origin (`OLLAMA_ORIGINS`).
- **Read and write with Claude.** Pick Anthropic Claude for text answers and image reading with your own key: the default model is the vision-capable one, the Base URL field changes the host the same key is sent to, the model list can be fetched, a check confirms the key, and images are sent as the base64 blocks the API takes. Calls from the browser carry Anthropic's browser-access header, without which the API refuses them.
- **Read French aloud with Fish Audio.** Choose Fish Audio for Speech synthesis with your own key: the free S2.1 Pro model is the default and no paid model is ever substituted for it, the voice ID is optional (leave it empty for the model's default voice), the model list can be fetched, and clips come back as MP3 and are kept like the others so they play again without another request.
- **Use an OpenAI-compatible provider with your own endpoint.** Pick OpenAI, or a compatible service such as DeepSeek or Qwen, for text answers and image reading with your own key; the Base URL field on Settings → AI services changes the host the same key is sent to, the model list can be fetched from that host, a wrong address is refused before anything is sent, and a key check confirms the address answers.
- **Read and shadow the exercise passage itself.** Open a unit’s passage in Reader to listen, look up words and sentences, prepare pronunciation markers and record it, even when its exercise batch has no prose. Return to the exercise sheet or reopen the right passage and sentence from My study after reload. Recordings are separate for each source, survive unit reset, and retain their provenance in optional backups; deleting its batch removes its passage recordings too.
- **Look up words while practising.** Tap words in exercise prompts, passages, hints and explanations to open the usual word sheet and save them to My study, using the current unit’s context. Word lookup keeps answer keyboard actions separate, and closing a word sheet returns to an open explanation.
- **Mistake notebook in My study.** Wrong answers are grouped by grammar area with their actual prompt and answers, kept after source deletion, and marked resolved after a later correct attempt. Explain a mistake, tap an actual word to look it up and save it, or reopen the surviving material/unit. Retry preserves the saved sheet; checked answers change only after its existing confirmed Reset.
- **Grammar progress and focused drills.** Progress shows weighted accuracy and real attempt counts by grammar area, including retained history from deleted material. Make a separate drill set targeting up to three weakest topics with errors, at your chosen level and size. Failed saves keep the generated set for retry without another AI call; leaving an unsaved set requires explicit discard.
- **Import an article from an image, PDF or pasted text.** Add on the library offers writing an article or importing one. Up to five images, or up to five pages of a PDF, are read by your OCR model into a title and text; pasted text uses no AI at all. You see the model and the pages before anything is sent, then correct the title, level and text and save one article, which opens in the Reader. The draft stays in memory, asks before you leave and survives a failed save for retry. Images and PDFs are prepared on this device with size and page limits, cancellation and cleanup, and PDF rendering uses bundled local resources rather than a CDN.
- **Import exercises from a worksheet.** Practice has an Import exercises page: choose an image, a PDF or pasted text, see the model and exactly what is sent, pick how missing answers are handled, then edit or exclude the extracted questions and answers, mark where an answer was printed or came from the AI, choose the level of the whole batch (required) and save. The result is one batch. The draft stays in memory, warns before you leave and survives a failed save for retry.
- **The last three exercise types, and Practice settings.** Building a sentence from word tiles (press the tiles in order, press a placed word to take it back; the arrangement that matches the reference is graded at once, any other is read by the AI, which writes feedback and never a score), reading passages (the passage sits beside the questions on a desktop and above them on a phone, with an "Open in the reader" link back into the material), and number dictation (a Listen button plays the number at your dictation speed and you type what you heard; the clip is generated once and kept). Settings gains a Practice section: strict accents (off by default, an answer without accents still counts), the dictation speed, and speaking a dictation item again after its check (off by default). Every control works with the keyboard and fits a 360 px phone.
- **Exercises from an article.** Practice (`#/practice`) lists an article's exercise sets with how far you are in each, and "Make exercises from this article" makes a new set at a level and number of items you choose; the reader has a "Make exercises" link that opens Practice for the article. Every item quotes the words of the article it rests on: an item whose quote is not in the article is left out, the page says how many ("3 items could not be checked against the article and were left out"), and the rest is kept; only when nothing is left does it fail, with the reason. Needs your key like the other AI features (it takes you to Settings → Text AI). All 11 exercise types are playable (fill in the blanks, choose the option, transform the word, open questions, gender checks, odd one out, matching pairs, ordering a dialogue, building a sentence from word tiles, completing a reading passage, writing numbers from dictation).
- **Four more exercise types on the sheet.** Gender checks (say whether a word is masculine or feminine), odd one out (pick the word that does not belong), matching pairs (pick the answer that goes with the question) and ordering a dialogue (arrange the lines with their up and down buttons) are now answered and checked like the others: a right pick is marked, a wrong one is struck through with the right answer beside it, and a dialogue line that sits in its right place stays plain while a misplaced one is struck through, with the right order listed below. Arrow keys move a pick or a line, so it all works without a mouse, and every button is large enough for a phone.
- **The exercise sheet.** Each set opens on one sheet (`#/practice/:unitId`, also listed under Practice in the sidebar): numbered rows with the blanks typed right in the sentence, choices as a small row of options, a Check button per row and "Check all" at the bottom. A right answer is marked; a wrong one is struck through with the right answer beside it. Your answers are saved as you type and come back after a reload; "Reset this unit" (after a confirmation) clears the sheet and keeps the history of your attempts. Checking never moves the page, and the sheet fits a 360 px phone in light, sepia and dark.
- **Hints, explanations and open answers with AI feedback.** "Hint" shows a nudge under the item without giving the answer away; "Explain" on a wrong answer opens a side sheet (bottom sheet on phones) with the rule and a tip. An open answer that does not match the sample is read by the AI, which writes feedback and a better version, never a score (it is an estimate, not an exam result). When the model's answer cannot be used, the message says so in your language with the reason.
- **Enter and a French character bar.** Enter moves to the next blank, then to an empty one, then checks; it never submits while you are composing text with an input method. A round "é" button at the bottom of the screen opens a small grid of é è ê ë à â ç î ï ô ù û ü œ æ « » (Shift for capitals) that types at the cursor without taking focus away from the answer.
- **Read aloud on the Practice page.** Step through the article's sentences, listen, record and get the AI's pronunciation score, in its own section.
- **New prompts in Settings → Prompts.** The prompt editor now lists the prompts for reading worksheets, reading article pages, exercise hints, mistake explanations, open-answer grading, adaptive drills and exercises made from an article, each editable in English and Chinese. Practice now sends the exercise, hint, explanation and open-answer prompts; worksheet import and adaptive drills use their own templates too.
- **Shadowing log.** In the reader, a microphone button next to each sentence opens a small practice card at the bottom of the screen: play the reference, record your repeat (with a live input level and a clock), redo, stop, then play back your take or compare it right after the reference. The last three takes of every sentence are kept on the device (the oldest goes first). The Vocabulary tab is now **My study**, with Words and Shadowing: the shadowing table lists each recorded sentence in article order with its reference audio and your recordings, opens the sentence in the reader, and deletes single takes. Recording pauses listening to the article and leaves it ready to resume. Settings shows how much recording space is used, deletes all recordings, and can include them in backups (off by default; a backup without them keeps the ones on the device). Recordings of an edited sentence stay under "original text changed"; deleting an article deletes its recordings. There is no scoring here yet. The card, the sentence bar and the article player sit on a dark ink panel that stands apart from the page in every theme. The card shows a waveform strip (your live input while recording, the real waveform of your latest take afterwards) above a centred Record button, with Reference on one side and Compare or Redo on the other. On desktop it can be dragged anywhere by its title; it reopens where you left it, and double-click (or Home) puts it back at the bottom. The card can be minimised to one row (sentence, record or stop with the clock, restore, close) so more of the article shows while you practise; it opens again by itself when something needs a decision. On phones and tablets the sentence actions (play, explain, practise) sit in a bar at the bottom of the screen instead of floating over the text, and the tapped sentence is scrolled clear of it.
- **Optional reader pronunciation markers.** Generate AI suggestions for connections, group-final stress, silent spelling and spoken syllables, then switch on the marker view once generation completes. Ordinary reading starts unchanged; layer toggles keep words in place, and lookup, copying and playback use the original text. Generation checks saved answers first, processes the article one sentence at a time, and Stop and Retry are always available. Validated answers are kept for 30 days, work offline without a key and are included in Settings' saved-answer counts, not backups. Frequent function words (je, le, les, est…) use a built-in pronunciation table, and a word the AI annotates inconsistently, such as a silent e in /ʒə/, is left unmarked instead of failing its whole sentence. A sentence whose AI answer still fails after one retry is skipped and stays plain, so the rest of the article can use markers; the panel shows how many were skipped, and Generate retries them. These are learning suggestions, not academy-certified annotations or audio alignment; uncertain text remains unmarked.
- **Listen to the whole article.** The reader's Listen button reads the article aloud sentence by sentence, highlighting and scrolling to the sentence being read. A small player floats at the bottom (it never pushes the text) with play/pause, previous/next sentence and stop; pausing works at any moment, even while a sentence is still loading. Audio is made one sentence at a time and only up to two sentences ahead, so playback starts quickly and pausing or stopping spends nothing more; every clip is kept and shared with the sentence buttons. Escape, playing a single sentence, or leaving the article ends listening.
- **Themes: light, sepia and dark**, plus "Match system", which follows the device and switches live. Pick one from the new theme icon in the header; the choice is remembered and applied before the page draws, so it never flashes. The whole app follows the theme, not only the article, and every text colour meets WCAG AA contrast in each theme.
- **Marketing site on GitHub Pages.** The Pages root is now an English and 简体中文 landing page (positioning, screenshots, FAQ, CTAs) with the live app unchanged under `/relire/app/`; a one-time root service worker retires the old root-scoped cache so returning visitors move over cleanly, and the app page asks search engines not to index it in favour of the landing pages.
- **Search and sharing metadata.** The app page now carries a proper title, description, canonical URL, robots directives, social-share images and Schema.org structured data, plus `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`, so search engines and shared links describe Relire correctly.
- **Live demo on GitHub Pages.** Pushing to main deploys https://royisme.github.io/relire/ (after the type, translation and build checks pass); the READMEs now lead with the demo and what Relire does, and a launch checklist with copy for communities lives in `docs/LAUNCH.md`.
- **Separate AI for explanations and for speech.** Settings has a provider, model and key for each, so they can differ; Gemini is the only provider today and the app is structured so others are added by implementing one interface and registering it. Each provider keeps its own key; unknown or invalid saved choices fall back to defaults.
- **Editable prompts.** Every prompt is now a plain-text template (English and Chinese) with variables and simple conditions. Settings shows them, previews them with sample values, warns about unknown variables, and resets any prompt to the original; edits are included in backups. Cached answers are keyed by the prompt in use, so an edited prompt gets fresh answers and resetting brings the old ones back.
- **Saved pronunciation audio.** Every synthesized clip is stored on the device and played from there, so it is generated once and works offline. Already acquired audio for bookmarked or introduced learner rows (word, personal sentences and personal usage-example French) is protected; unseen book words and built-in examples are not. Other clips are trimmed least-recently-used past 200 MiB. Settings shows the amount and can clear the rest. Backups can optionally include the vocabulary's audio; if some clips cannot be saved while restoring, Settings says how many.
- **Saved explanations with the word.** Saving keeps the request-language explanation and examples as a separately attributed personal context; audio is explicit only.
- **Saved AI answers.** Word analyses are kept until cleared (no entry limit), sentence analyses for 30 days, so repeating them costs nothing and cached answers work without a key. Identical requests in flight are shared. Settings shows the counts and can clear them.
- **Progress page: French sounds in IPA.** A tap-to-hear chart of vowels, nasal vowels, semi-vowels and consonants with an example word each.
- **Article library.** The Read tab opens a table of articles with search, level filter, sortable columns, add, edit and delete (with confirmation) and a way to restore the sample articles. Opening an article shows the reader with a back link.
- **First-run guide.** Three steps: welcome, connect Gemini (the key is checked before it is saved), try a sample article. Skipping leaves a banner until a key is set.
- **Navigation.** Flat tabs from tablet width up and a left drawer on phones; the interface language is a single icon with a menu.
- **IndexedDB storage** for articles, vocabulary and stats, with row-owned durable article/vocabulary writes and debounced stats saves, visible real failures, storage use and a "Protect my data" request in Settings.
- **Translation check** (`bun run i18n:check`, run in CI) that fails on missing or mismatched keys and hard-coded Chinese text.
- Repository basics: MIT licence, English and Chinese README, PRODUCT, DESIGN and CLAUDE guides, CI that type-checks, checks translations and builds.

### Changed
- Learning navigation now separates Reading, Grammar, Listening and Speaking, with Writing shown for saved writing units only. Grammar reference and exercises are linked; Read aloud has durable article/sentence addresses. Imports confirm each unit's explicit learning area, mixed sets stay whole, and deletion names its complete cross-area scope. Attempts retain their area and grammar accuracy uses grammar history only. Old unreleased unit/attempt/backup shapes are intentionally rejected, without migration or data reset.
- Browser speech recognition while recording is now an opt-in setting in Settings → Practice, off by default. It may send the recording to the browser maker's speech service, so it only runs when you turn it on.
- Words use FSRS-6 with Forgot/Fuzzy/Remembered recall and a target-retention setting (80–95%, default90%). Reviews keep their actual submission time through save retries; per-card resets preserve history and create New memory without extra reset markers. Backups record scheduler policy once, not on every review. Prior unreleased FSRS rows/backups with the removed fields are rejected, with no automatic conversion or learner-data reset.
- **Word settings live in Settings, and the Words badge counts words.** Settings has a new Words section (`#/settings/words`) holding the new-words-per-day limit and the built-in wordbook's download, update, count and failure state, moved off Today; an unsaved limit survives switching Settings sections and is dropped when you leave Settings. Today keeps its real counts and Start/continue. The sidebar's due count now sits on the Words row instead of the My study row, beside Mistakes' own unresolved count, so the two numbers are never added together; the icon rail shows no child count. The review page no longer repeats the 1/2/3 recall instructions, though the keys and the labelled buttons still work.
- Words cards now use a narrow portrait paper layout with subtle depth, generous word whitespace and clearer meanings/example pairs. Clicking either face still flips, with selectable answers and independent Listen; three equal circular Forgot/Fuzzy/Remembered controls use the existing colour palette and save recall as ratings 1/3/4, without visible Flip buttons or stacked answer headings.
- Words review now uses a two-way flipping card with separate Forgot/Remembered actions, no pre-answer rating dashboard, and full readable answers. First reveal still saves before showing the answer; later flips never change progress or count as recall.
- Owner-authorized local vocabulary use can explicitly waive linguistic review while retaining unreviewed provenance and all structural/hash/publication checks; reviewed publication remains the default and no public delivery rights are granted.
- Word lookup has explicit Save word / Save this context / Remove bookmark actions with frozen source and request language. Saving no longer prefetches speech. Vocabulary analytics report bookmarks, introductions and self-ratings, not mastery; optional audio backup/protection includes only acquired personal clips for bookmarked or introduced cards.
- **A clearer word sheet and Conjugation page.** The word sheet names a tapped verb form in French (`aller : présent, je`), keeps the whole conjugation behind "Show all tenses" with the tapped form underlined, and its Conjugation link is a button; the Conjugation page no longer says "regular -er verbs" above an irregular verb, marks a differing form (the rule’s in green, the model’s struck through) and the tense that has one, keeps its Patterns rows to the height they need, and the four My study tabs fit a 360 px phone.
- **Practice is a table of exercise sets.** Search, filter by level and sort real progress; open a set to see its exercises, delete an exercise or the whole set after a named confirmation, and keep attempt history in Mistakes. Add exercises groups worksheet import and article generation in one dialog, with Read aloud below the table; Library shares the same table foundation without changing its controls.
- **Importing is split into an article import and an exercise import.** The single Import page is gone. Read has "Import an article" (`#/import/article`) and Practice has "Import exercises" (`#/import/exercises`), each as its own sidebar entry; the library's Add dialog opens the article import and Practice has an Import exercises button. A worksheet is saved as exercises only, never as an article or as both, and the exercise import no longer shows a source-text field.
- **Exercise sets stand on their own.** Practice now lists your exercise sets by name and level, each opening its units, and no longer follows an article: imported worksheets (saved with the level you choose), sets made from an article and drill sets are all self-contained, so deleting an article leaves its sets alone and a mistake no longer links back to an article. A set without a title is named by its exercise type and the start of its id.
- **The app is now called Relire Plus.** The tab title, install prompt, landing pages and READMEs use the new name; nothing about your data or settings changes.
- **The "Speak after checking" setting says what it does.** It is now "Speak dictation after checking", because only a dictation item is spoken again after its check; no other exercise type speaks its answer.
- **One AI services page.** Settings now has a single AI services section with three linked pages — Text AI, OCR and Speech synthesis — each with its own provider and model under one page-wide Save, and one key per provider that every page reuses (noted on each page that shares it). Fetch the provider's model list with the key you are typing, or type any model ID; a list is discovery only, and a model whose image or speech support is unconfirmed says so. Check key and Preview voice use the settings on screen and never save. A save that the browser refuses is reported, a model ID that storage cannot keep stops the save instead of being replaced by a default, and unsaved AI changes ask before you leave Settings. When a feature needs a key, it opens the matching page with the key field ready, a notice naming that page's task and a way back to what you were doing; importing an image or PDF names the OCR page and keeps the draft until you discard it.
- **Independent image-reading configuration.** Image/PDF extraction uses its own OCR selection, while pasted text uses Text AI; consent pins the selected recipient and key. Custom model IDs survive reload, and model discovery keeps unconfirmed image/speech capabilities distinct from known support. Speech previews check the current draft rather than reusing an old clip.
- **Clearer study navigation and About.** About is now the last sidebar destination, outside Settings; old About links still work. Collapse or expand the sidebar with its own icon at the top. My study shows page links locally only on phones or with the icon rail, without repeating the expanded sidebar’s links.
- **Sentence-first shadowing history.** Each full-width sentence now has its reference, recording playback, Compare and clearly named All recordings actions underneath, with the last recording’s date and duration kept as quiet metadata. Expanding the history uses the full width and keeps playback and deletion available even for a single take.
- **Chinese copy for the new screens checked.** The mistake notebook, grammar progress and passage reading now use the app's existing Chinese terms (文章库 for the library, 加入生词本 for saving a word, 重置本组 for resetting a unit) instead of new synonyms.
- **"Drills" is now "Practice"** in the sidebar, the menu and the page itself, in both languages. Settings' saved-answer counts now include saved exercise help instead of drill sets.
- **A sidebar instead of top tabs, and Settings as a page.** From tablet width, a sidebar replaces the tabs: an icon rail between 768 and 1024 px, and from 1024 px a column with a label for each place, the sub-pages of the one you are in (My study: Words and Shadowing; Settings: its sections), the number of words due, and Settings and Install app at the bottom. On wide screens it collapses to the rail (remembered), and in the reader it always starts collapsed so the article keeps the page. On phones the menu drawer shows the same list. The header now holds only the name, theme and language. Settings is a page with Text AI, Speech, Prompts, Data and backup, and About; each section has its own Save button and shows "Saved" or the reason it failed, instead of one Save for everything. When something needs your key, Relire opens Settings → Text AI with the key field ready, and after you check and save the key, one button takes you back to where you were. Every place has its own address, so Back and Forward work, a reload keeps your place (the article you were reading, the My study tab, the settings section), and an unknown address or a deleted article shows a page that leads back to the library.
- **Deleting an article finishes in storage first.** It leaves the library only after storage confirms, so a failed delete no longer looks successful.
- **Calmer, plainer interface.** Rewrote titles and labels in both languages without hype, jargon, emoji or exclamation marks (for example "Vocabulary" instead of "SRS Vocabulary Deck (Spaced Repetition)", "Drills" instead of "Interactive Reinforcement Drills"). The word and sentence sheets read like a dictionary entry: headings and rules instead of tinted cards and sparkle icons. Every tab uses the same header; filters and modes use one segmented control; one filled button per view. Settings lost its numbered titles, decorative icons and coloured info boxes. Model and voice descriptions are now plain.
- **Shadowing section redesigned.** In the sentence sheet, "Shadowing" is now one clear block: how to say it (pace, phrasing as chips, liaisons, intonation, each label above its text), then Record, play back your recording, and Check pronunciation, then the result (overall score, accuracy / fluency / rhythm, sounds to work on with a status icon, words to fix, notes). The long "AI Shadowing Coach & Pronunciation Evaluation" title, its repeated subtitle and the off-palette colours are gone, and the record button is readable again. Practice's read-aloud section uses the same component, so the two behave alike. Mic and scoring errors show inline with the real reason instead of an alert, a new recording clears the old result, and closing the sheet releases the microphone. - **Code structure:** the AI layer is split into providers, prompt templates and tasks; the speech code into stored clips, playback and recording; Settings into a shell plus AI, prompts and data sections. Behaviour is unchanged (the default prompts are word-for-word the previous ones).
- **Renamed to Relire** ("to read again"), with a new icon.
- **Runs entirely in the browser.** The Express server is gone; Gemini is called directly with the user's own key, which stays in the browser. The build is a static PWA that can be hosted anywhere and installed. Bun is the only package manager.
- **Playing audio no longer moves the page.** The "now playing" bar that pushed the article down on every play is gone. Loading and playing show on the button you pressed (speaker, spinner, stop square) at a fixed size, and in the reader the playing sentence is highlighted. Escape, or leaving the article, stops the clip; stopping while a clip is still loading now really cancels it, and starting another clip replaces it. The reader's speed panel floats over the article instead of pushing it down. This covers the reader, word and sentence sheets, practice, vocabulary and the IPA chart.
- **Sentence actions float over the text** as a compact pill that follows the reading theme (white, parchment, sepia, dark) and sits at the end of the sentence — directly below its last line, flipping above it near the bottom of the screen — so reading no longer shifts while hovering. On touch, tapping a word keeps the sentence's actions visible after the lookup sheet closes.
- **Design language unified:** one ink-blue accent, warm neutral paper, Source Serif 4 for reading and Public Sans for the interface, a 68-character reading column, one radius and elevation scheme. Word and sentence lookups open in a side sheet (bottom sheet on phones); tasks that need focus use a dialog.

- Model, voice and level names, service errors and remaining labels moved into the locale files; Chinese copy tidied (no French parentheticals, no "Mac" wording); page title and `lang` follow the language.
