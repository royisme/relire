# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Register

**Read** for the reader, **Operate** for vocabulary, practice, analytics and settings. Nothing in the app is Persuade; there is no marketing surface.

## What it is

Relire (French for "to read again") is a close-reading assistant for French learners preparing for TCF Canada, with a Chinese and English interface. The learner reads real French articles and taps any word or sentence to get an AI-generated breakdown from Gemini. Words they save feed a spaced-repetition deck, and they practise pronunciation aloud against an AI assessor. It is a static PWA: no app server, no account. The learner supplies their own API key (Gemini today), stored in the browser and used for direct calls to the provider. One key per provider serves every AI task that names it, while text lookups, image and PDF reading, and speech each choose their own model independently. User data (articles, vocabulary, stats, saved answers and audio, shadowing recordings) lives in the browser's IndexedDB; settings and keys live in localStorage.

## Direction: Relire Plus

Relire is absorbing FrenchLab AI so a learner does all of their study in one app: self-contained exercise sets, each with its own level and an optional title (11 types, including dictation and AI-graded open answers), importing articles from images, PDFs or text and exercises from worksheet pages, a mistake notebook, and drills for weak grammar areas, beside reading and shadowing. Scope therefore widens to listening and writing practice; there are still no mock exams and no official scores. The audience widens to A1–C1. The display name becomes Relire Plus in phase 6. Everything stays static and BYOK. The approved plan, its phases and the decisions behind them are in `docs/merge-plan.md`; until a phase ships, the sections below describe the product as it is.

## Positioning

Real French articles read sentence by sentence, where every sentence can be heard, repeated aloud and kept as a recording to compare against the reference later. The learner brings their own AI key; there is no account, and everything stays on their device.

## Users

Intermediate learners (roughly A2–C1) preparing for TCF/DELF/DALF or reading French for pleasure. They are mostly Chinese speakers on a laptop or phone, in sessions of 15–45 minutes, often in the evening. They are already reading in a second language, so extra visual noise costs them real attention.

## Core jobs, in priority order

1. **Read an article without losing the thread.** The text is the product. Chrome recedes.
2. **Understand a word or sentence in context, then return to reading.** Lookups are brief detours: word (meaning, IPA, conjugation, examples), sentence (syntax segments, grammar points, shadowing guide).
3. **Keep what was learned.** Save words with separately attributed contexts; introduce on first answer reveal, then review with a two-way word/answer card and explicit Forgot/Fuzzy/Remembered feedback, FSRS-6 scheduling with short learning steps and local due-day reviews (self-reported recall, not correctness).
4. **Shadow a sentence and keep the record.** Hear a sentence, repeat it right away, and keep the last few takes per sentence to replay and compare with the reference at any time. No score is required.
5. **Speak and be corrected.** Optional AI assessment: a score plus phoneme, liaison and intonation feedback (open practice and the sentence sheet today; an optional score for shadowing takes later, switched in Settings).
6. **See progress honestly.** Counts and scores from real data only.

## Screens

First run: a three-step guide (welcome, connect Gemini with a checked key, try a sample article). Skipping is allowed and leaves a persistent banner until a key is set.


- **Library** (the Read tab's home): an article data table on the same foundation as area exercises, with search, level filter, sortable columns, add, edit and confirmed delete. An article opens Reader.
- **Import an article** (Read): up to five images or five selected PDF pages are read in order into a title and text by the OCR model (explicit model/page consent), pasted text uses no AI; the learner corrects the title, level and text, then saves one article that opens in the Reader. Drafts are in memory and warn before leaving.
- **Import exercises** (from a learning area's Add exercises): up to five images, five selected PDF pages or pasted worksheet text, explicit model/content consent, editable required learning area per unit, question review and inclusion, then atomic save as one batch with the level the learner chooses (required), opening the new exercise set. Never saved as an article. Drafts are in memory and warn before leaving.
- **Reader**: an article or a distinct exercise-unit passage, back to the library or its exercise sheet, reading settings (font size, playback speed), "Make exercises" (opens Reading exercises, where an article can be picked), listen to the whole article, optional pronunciation markers, tap-to-analyse words, sentence toolbar (play, analyse, record). Record opens a closable practice card docked at the bottom: reference, record/stop, redo, input level, latest take with play and compare.
- **Word detail**: translation, IPA, tense, usage examples, save to vocabulary. For a verb in the app's reviewed list the sheet shows its conjugation table from that list (no AI, nothing guessed); for any other verb it shows one "Conjugate this verb" link to the Conjugation page with the verb filled in. A "Verb form" line sits directly under the meaning and names the verb, tense and person in French when the tapped word is a form in the reviewed list (`vais`: `aller : présent, je`), nothing when it is not; the sheet is drawn only once the pack has loaded, so that line and the conjugation never arrive late. The table is closed behind "Show all tenses" and marks the tapped form. The AI no longer writes a table. Noun entries show one qualified ending tendency under the meaning, using the lemma rather than a tapped plural and the longest matching reviewed ending. Known A1 exceptions are labelled; an unknown noun's gender is not inferred. Both packs settle before the entry mounts, and failures keep their real text.
- **Sentence drawer**: translation, syntax, grammar points, collocations, shadowing record and score.
- **My study**: the learner's own material, in three parts. **Words**: Today (`#/study/words`) shows actual due/learning counts, remaining daily introductions and Start/continue. Settings → Words owns the new-word limit (default 20, integer 0–100), target retention (default 90%, integer 80–95%) and the built-in wordbook's download, update, count, progress and failure state. Retention is a model target, not a measured score or guarantee; higher targets mean more review work and changes apply only to subsequent saved reviews. All words (`/all`) joins the complete downloaded book and personal rows, searches/filters and paginates at 50. Review (`/review`) uses a two-way flipping card, French first, and commits introduction before mounting any answer. Later flips are display-only. Click either card face to flip, with keyboard access and no visible Flip buttons; selecting text, scrolling and Listen remain independent. Meanings appear directly, without stacked Answer/Built-in headings. Outside the card, three equal circular icon targets labelled Forgot/Fuzzy/Remembered appear only on the unlocked back, using existing bad/warm-neutral/ok colours and recording semantic outcomes through the single FSRS-6 scheduler (ts-fsrs 5.4.2). Fuzzy means recall before flipping with hesitation or effort; recognition only after seeing the answer belongs to Forgot. Easy is not offered or inferred. Flipping and Skip never count as remembered. Introduction leaves New memory with zero reviews; short steps really wait. Saved snapshots work without a key or service. Save this context and Remove bookmark are distinct; removal keeps contexts/history. Full learner-card deletion and schedule reset ask separately. Saving, downloading, revealing and rating never call AI or speech automatically. **Shadowing**: every recorded sentence by article or unit passage, as text | reference audio | my recordings, with compare, delete and open in the reader. **Mistakes** (`#/study/mistakes`): the latest wrong answer per exercise item, grouped by grammar area, with the actual prompt, given and reference answers and a resolved marker after a later correct attempt. Explanations use the saved attempt and the surviving unit context, if available. Tap a word to look it up and save it through the usual word sheet; retry opens the saved unit without resetting it. A deleted unit keeps its readable history but has no retry link.
- **Grammar** (`#/grammar`, optional `?tag=`): Topics, Conjugation, Gender and saved Exercises are linked destinations outside My study; Conjugation and Gender need no content service. Topics are grouped by chapter with levels and a dismissible tag filter; a reader shows the interface language's prose and keeps French examples, with tables scrolling inside the page on phones. No topics are published yet, so the list says so. **Conjugation** (`#/grammar/conjugation`, optional `?verb=`): type a verb in the infinitive and see every tense with when it is used and how it is formed, the auxiliary, the participles and, for a verb the AI answered, one example sentence per tense labelled AI-made. A verb in the app's reviewed list is answered from it with no AI call and no key and is called Reviewed; any other verb is asked of the AI with the learner's key and compared with the app's own rules, and is only ever called "matches the rule" (not human-reviewed), "some forms differ" (a cell then shows the model's form and the rule's form) or "AI-made, not checked", never verified. Failures, including a missing key, show their real reason beside the input. A Patterns section below the tenses lets the learner choose a tense and an available person, then shows negation, an est-ce que question and an inversion question without another AI call. Its three rows stay in place; an unavailable pattern says why, and unchecked or differing source cells keep a note. The pattern rules await language review. A "Practise this verb" button, shown when some forms can be asked (reviewed or matching the rule), makes one exercise set of all seven tenses from the displayed verb with no AI, and then links to it.
- **Area exercises**: Reading (`#/read/exercises`), Grammar (`#/grammar/exercises`), Listening (`#/listening`) and Writing (`#/writing`) each have a searchable, level-filtered, sortable table of exercise sets. Writing's menu entry exists only when a saved writing unit exists; it reuses the current controls, not a new writing product. Units have an explicit primary `learningArea`, independent of interaction type, grammar tag, section and import source. The list counts only units in its area before aggregating sets, newest first, named by title or by exercise type and the start of the id. Each row shows its level, exercise count, real checked-item progress and added date, without set deletion on filtered rows. A set opens its complete original batch, including other areas with visible area labels and whole-batch totals. Unit and set addresses carry their entered area (`#/{read|grammar|listening|writing}/exercises/unit/:unitId` and `/set/:setId`); wrong-area units or batches with no matching entry-area unit show not found. Whole-set deletion lives here, naming its real cross-area count, answers and passage recordings. The exercise table links each unit through its actual area and keeps confirmed per-exercise deletion; deleting the last exercise also removes the set. Attempts remain in the mistake notebook. One Add exercises action opens an area-specific dialog. Every area can import a worksheet at `#/import/exercises?area=<learningArea>` and confirm editable per-unit areas, including mixed batches. Reading and Grammar can generate from an article at an explicit requested goal, level and size; the returned areas must match that goal. Failed saves retry the same generated payload and identities. Only Grammar offers reviewed conjugation drills. Listening supports NUMBER_DICTATION with dictation text on every effective item, not general audio comprehension or prompt TTS. Writing supports saved imported units without a new generator. One unit is one sheet: numbered rows with typed blanks, inline choices, option picks (gender, odd one out, matching), ordered dialogue lines, word tiles that build a sentence, a reading passage that sits beside its questions, a dictation Listen button and open answers, a check per row and "Check all", hints, explanations of wrong answers, open answers judged by an AI in words (an estimate, never a score), a French character bar, and a unit reset that keeps the history. Words in prompts, reading passages, hints and explanations open the usual word sheet and can be saved to vocabulary, with the current unit’s instructions and passage as context, never an unrelated last-read article. Enter or Space on a word opens lookup without checking an answer; answer fields keep their Enter and composition rules. Answer-choice buttons and word tiles keep their answer actions. A passage opens its exact stored text in the same Reader for listening, word and sentence analysis, pronunciation markers and shadowing, with its own level and the set's name as source. Passage recordings belong to the unit and stay separate from article prose and other units, keep their source and sentence on return from My study or reload, and can be included in backups. Unit reset keeps them; changed or removed passage text keeps playable history without a broken source link; deleting its set removes its passage recordings too. Reading a passage never creates a Library copy. All 11 exercise types are playable (fill in the blanks, choose the option, transform the word, open questions, gender checks, odd one out, matching pairs, ordering a dialogue, building a sentence from word tiles, completing a reading passage, writing numbers from dictation). Speaking is separate: `#/speaking` offers Read aloud and explains reader shadowing; `#/speaking/read-aloud` chooses an article and `#/speaking/read-aloud/:articleId?sentence=...` opens its existing recording/assessment workspace. Missing or changed selected sources are unavailable, never replaced by the first article. Sentence navigation survives reload and Back, and pronunciation estimates retain their original separate statistics.
- **Progress**: three plain figures (words saved, average pronunciation score, shadowing sessions), recent scores, and a tap-to-hear chart of the French sounds in IPA. Grammar accuracy shows real correct/total counts from explicitly grammar-classified attempts only, by attempted grammar area, weighted by attempt counts rather than averaging topic percentages, including retained history from deleted sources. One action makes a standalone drill set (no article) targeting up to three weakest nonperfect grammar topics, at the chosen level and size, titled with the drill's own title. The set saves in one step; a failed save retains the generated set for storage-only retry without another AI call, and leaving an unsaved set requires explicit discard. No coaching text or invented scores.
- **Settings**: a page with five sections: AI services, Practice (strict accents, dictation speed, speaking after a check), Words (daily new-word limit, target retention and the built-in wordbook download), editable prompts, stored data (saved answers, audio, shadowing recordings) with backup/restore JSON (recordings optional). About is a separate page. AI services is one page with three destinations — Text AI, OCR, Speech synthesis — that each hold a provider and its independently chosen model, one key per provider shared by every destination that names it, and one page-wide Save for all three. The model can be fetched from the provider with the unsaved key and searched, or typed by ID; a fetched list is discovery only, and a model whose support is unconfirmed says so. Each destination can check the key (or preview the voice) without saving; changing a selection never changes another destination's. Practice, prompts and data each save on their own. Language and theme are single icons in the header.

### Noun gender reference and drills

Grammar → Gender (`#/grammar/gender`) shows all 17 reviewed ending tendencies grouped by predicted gender, with measured dictionary and reviewed A1 agreement counts and percentages, French examples and A1-sample exceptions. The intentional `-ure` override keeps its actual 902/1216 dictionary and 12/12 A1 figures. No ending is a guarantee, and an empty exception sample is not an exhaustive dictionary claim.

One Make exercises form samples 1–337 reviewed A1 nouns without replacement, including exceptions, defaulting to eight items and level A1 with a free A1–C1 level choice. One standalone gender-check unit saves through the existing exercise flow; a failed save retries the same sample and identities. The page links to the saved set. Each checked item reveals its saved longest-ending tendency and any reviewed exception note in the current interface language, without fetching the pack again. Reference, drill creation and deterministic checking need no AI, key or service; ordinary word lookup retains its usual AI workflow.

### Grammar learning pilot

The first-chapter pilot is noun gender/feminine forms and noun plurals only. A topic with authored questions shows compact teaching, then three recognition-and-typed pairs, one question at a time. All six questions and the initially closed full reference are freely accessible; checking never advances automatically. Accents always count. Instructions and short verdict/distractor feedback follow the interface language, with longer feedback in a sheet. No AI key, runtime generation/grading, lookup, speech or recording is used in this flow, including saved grammar exercises and Mistakes.

Opening only reads saved exercise state; the first answer saves one revision reused by Grammar teaching and its saved exercises. Redo/reset keep attempts; Mistakes retains the original authored feedback even after deletion. Backups preserve those private snapshots. Learning, feedback and compact-teaching edits create a new revision; unrelated full-reference edits do not. Counts describe current checked/right answers, not mastery or independent recall; skipping/reference use is not tracked. Topics without learning remain reference readers.

Runtime and private authoring/review/publication code are verified with synthetic fixtures only. Real twelve-question language review and local acceptance remain pending; this is not a claim of reviewed or published learning content.

### Vocabulary code versus content

The private explicit-source normalizer groups canonical spellings, records corrections and entry/sense/example omissions, reports quality issues and requires actual hash-bound review before local staged publication. Runtime downloads validate every pinned page and the complete aggregate before one durable offline cache. The service intentionally permits complete-book download; rate limits do not guarantee completion or prevent collection. No real corpus was imported, reviewed, applied or published by this implementation. C1 omission method, target-relevant Canadian usage, corrected artifact/reviewer and distribution approval remain owner decisions. Personal words are never subject to inferred C1 filtering. Paired backups retain learner rows, preferences and all daily ledgers, not disposable book pages or invalidation tokens.

### Classification and history boundaries

Each saved exercise unit and each attempt requires `learningArea` (`reading|grammar|listening|writing`). Producers set it explicitly: topics, conjugation, gender and adaptive grammar drills are grammar; article generation requests reading or grammar; import reviews every suggested area. Mistakes visibly retain area identity after source deletion and Retry uses the surviving unit's actual area. The shared stores, atomic checks, reset tokens and retained history remain unchanged. Old unreleased shapes and backups fail validation intentionally; this change performs no migration, reset or real learner-data operation. Old Practice and Study/Grammar routes are removed without aliases. Ordinary Settings → Practice preferences remain shared exercise settings.

## Tone and personality

A quiet, well-edited reading room: calm, literate, precise. French printed matter is the reference (a good paperback, a dictionary), not a gamified language app. Encouraging in copy but never cute; errors name the problem and the way out.

## Anti-references

Duolingo-style gamification (streak flames, confetti, mascots), generic AI-dashboard styling (gradients, glass, glowing cards, rainbow icons, hero-metric tiles, emoji as icons), and anything that makes the article column feel like one widget among many.

## Design principles

1. **The article is the page.** Reading column 60–70ch, generous leading, a real text face. Everything else is secondary and quieter.
2. **Lookups are detours, not destinations.** Contextual lookups open in a side sheet (bottom sheet on phones) beside the text and never hide it. Centered modals are reserved for tasks that need focus: the first-run guide, import, delete, install. Settings is a page.
3. **One accent, used for action and the current selection.** Colour beyond that is semantic only: success, error.
4. **Show real data or nothing.** No invented scores, streaks or levels; empty states say what to do next.
5. **Touch and keyboard are first-class.** Hover never gates a feature; targets are at least 40px on touch; focus is always visible; motion is short and respects reduced-motion.
6. **Chinese and French typeset together.** Layouts tolerate CJK and Latin mixed lines, long French words and translated strings of different length.

## Constraints

React 19, Vite, Tailwind v4, lucide-react icons, i18next (en/zh), Bun. Fully client-side: AI providers are called from the browser with the user's own keys, and every feature asks one access boundary (`src/services/ai/access.ts`) for its purpose's selection and execution; prompts are editable templates, not code. That boundary is the stated seam where a hosted, paid service could later be supplied — the browser would then reach it instead of the provider — but no account, plan, balance or remote usage exists in this delivery. The one backend is an optional read-only content service for grammar, lexicon and wordbook content (`docs/adr-hosted-content-service.md`): it never sees a key, an answer, an article or a recording (it receives only a topic id, tapped word or book id/page/version), and the app works without it. Client-only persistence: user data in IndexedDB (`src/storage`), settings and API key in localStorage (`relire_*`). Never invent data to fill an empty state.
