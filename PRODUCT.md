# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Register

**Read** for the reader, **Operate** for vocabulary, practice, analytics and settings. Nothing in the app is Persuade; there is no marketing surface.

## What it is

Relire (French for "to read again") is a close-reading assistant for French learners preparing for TCF Canada, with a Chinese and English interface. The learner reads real French articles and taps any word or sentence to get an AI-generated breakdown from Gemini. Words they save feed a spaced-repetition deck, and they practise pronunciation aloud against an AI assessor. It is a static PWA: no app server, no account. The learner supplies their own API key (Gemini today), stored in the browser and used for direct calls to the provider. One key per provider serves every AI task that names it, while text lookups, image and PDF reading, and speech each choose their own model independently. User data (articles, vocabulary, stats, saved answers and audio, shadowing recordings) lives in the browser's IndexedDB; settings and keys live in localStorage.

## Direction: Relire Plus

Relire is absorbing FrenchLab AI so a learner does all of their study in one app: self-contained exercise sets, each with its own level and an optional title (11 types, including dictation and AI-graded open answers), importing articles from images, PDFs or text and exercises from worksheet pages, a mistake notebook, and drills for weak grammar areas, beside reading and shadowing. Scope therefore widens to listening and writing practice; there are still no mock exams and no official scores. The audience widens to A1–C1. The display name becomes Relire Plus in phase 6. Everything stays static and BYOK. The approved plan, its phases and the decisions behind them are in `docs/merge-plan.md`; until a phase ships, the sections below describe the product as it is.

## Positioning

Real French articles read sentence by sentence, where every sentence can be heard, repeated aloud and kept as a recording to compare against the reference later. The learner brings their own AI key; there is no account, and everything stays on their device.

## Users

Intermediate learners (roughly A2–C1) preparing for TCF/DELF/DALF or reading French for pleasure. They are mostly Chinese speakers on a laptop or phone, in sessions of 15–45 minutes, often in the evening. They are already reading in a second language, so extra visual noise costs them real attention.

## Core jobs, in priority order

1. **Read an article without losing the thread.** The text is the product. Chrome recedes.
2. **Understand a word or sentence in context, then return to reading.** Lookups are brief detours: word (meaning, IPA, conjugation, examples), sentence (syntax segments, grammar points, shadowing guide).
3. **Keep what was learned.** Save words with separately attributed contexts; introduce on first answer reveal, then review with two minute-learning steps and an SM-2-style day schedule (self-ratings 1/3/4/5, not correctness).
4. **Shadow a sentence and keep the record.** Hear a sentence, repeat it right away, and keep the last few takes per sentence to replay and compare with the reference at any time. No score is required.
5. **Speak and be corrected.** Optional AI assessment: a score plus phoneme, liaison and intonation feedback (open practice and the sentence sheet today; an optional score for shadowing takes later, switched in Settings).
6. **See progress honestly.** Counts and scores from real data only.

## Screens

First run: a three-step guide (welcome, connect Gemini with a checked key, try a sample article). Skipping is allowed and leaves a persistent banner until a key is set.


- **Library** (the Read tab's home): an article data table on the same foundation as Practice, with search, level filter, sortable columns, add, edit and confirmed delete. An article opens Reader.
- **Import an article** (Read): up to five images or five selected PDF pages are read in order into a title and text by the OCR model (explicit model/page consent), pasted text uses no AI; the learner corrects the title, level and text, then saves one article that opens in the Reader. Drafts are in memory and warn before leaving.
- **Import exercises** (Practice): up to five images, five selected PDF pages or pasted worksheet text, explicit model/content consent, editable question review and inclusion, then atomic save as one batch with the level the learner chooses (required), opening the new exercise set. Never saved as an article. Drafts are in memory and warn before leaving.
- **Reader**: an article or a distinct exercise-unit passage, back to the library or its exercise sheet, reading settings (font size, playback speed), "Make exercises" (opens Practice, where an article is picked), listen to the whole article, optional pronunciation markers, tap-to-analyse words, sentence toolbar (play, analyse, record). Record opens a closable practice card docked at the bottom: reference, record/stop, redo, input level, latest take with play and compare.
- **Word detail**: translation, IPA, tense, usage examples, save to vocabulary. For a verb in the app's reviewed list the sheet shows its conjugation table from that list (no AI, nothing guessed); for any other verb it shows one "Conjugate this verb" link to the Conjugation page with the verb filled in. A "Verb form" line sits directly under the meaning and names the verb, tense and person in French when the tapped word is a form in the reviewed list (`vais`: `aller : présent, je`), nothing when it is not; the sheet is drawn only once the pack has loaded, so that line and the conjugation never arrive late. The table is closed behind "Show all tenses" and marks the tapped form. The AI no longer writes a table.
- **Sentence drawer**: translation, syntax, grammar points, collocations, shadowing record and score.
- **My study**: the learner's own material, in four parts. **Words**: Today (`#/study/words`) shows actual due/learning counts and remaining daily introductions (default 20, explicit limit 0–100). All words (`/all`) joins the complete downloaded book and personal rows, searches/filters and paginates at 50. Review (`/review`) shows French first, commits introduction before revealing any answer, then accepts Again/Hard/Good/Easy. Short steps really wait; mature Hard/Good/Easy share an immediate interval and may affect later ease. Saved snapshots work without a key or service. Save this context and Remove bookmark are distinct; removal keeps contexts/history. Full learner-card deletion and schedule reset ask separately. Saving, downloading, revealing and rating never call AI or speech automatically. **Shadowing**: every recorded sentence by article or unit passage, as text | reference audio | my recordings, with compare, delete and open in the reader. **Mistakes** (`#/study/mistakes`): the latest wrong answer per exercise item, grouped by grammar area, with the actual prompt, given and reference answers and a resolved marker after a later correct attempt. Explanations use the saved attempt and the surviving unit context, if available. Tap a word to look it up and save it through the usual word sheet; retry opens the saved unit without resetting it. A deleted unit keeps its readable history but has no retry link. **Grammar** (`#/study/grammar`, optional `?tag=`): Topics and Conjugation behind a section switch. Topics are grouped by chapter with levels and a dismissible tag filter; a reader shows the interface language's prose and keeps French examples, with tables scrolling inside the page on phones. No topics are published yet, so the list says so. **Conjugation** (`#/study/grammar/conjugation`, optional `?verb=`): type a verb in the infinitive and see every tense with when it is used and how it is formed, the auxiliary, the participles and, for a verb the AI answered, one example sentence per tense labelled AI-made. A verb in the app's reviewed list is answered from it with no AI call and no key and is called Reviewed; any other verb is asked of the AI with the learner's key and compared with the app's own rules, and is only ever called "matches the rule" (not human-reviewed), "some forms differ" (a cell then shows the model's form and the rule's form) or "AI-made, not checked", never verified. Failures, including a missing key, show their real reason beside the input. A Patterns section below the tenses lets the learner choose a tense and an available person, then shows negation, an est-ce que question and an inversion question without another AI call. Its three rows stay in place; an unavailable pattern says why, and unchecked or differing source cells keep a note. The pattern rules await language review. A "Practise this verb" button, shown when some forms can be asked (reviewed or matching the rule), makes one exercise set of all seven tenses from the displayed verb with no AI, and then links to it.
- **Practice**: a searchable, level-filtered, sortable table of exercise sets, newest first, named by title or by exercise type and the start of the id. Each row shows its level, exercise count, real checked-item progress and added date, with confirmed set deletion. A set opens its own page with an exercise table, links to each sheet and confirmed per-exercise deletion; deleting the last exercise also removes the set. Attempts remain in the mistake notebook. Add exercises opens a dialog to import a worksheet, make a set from an article (pick an article, a level and a number of items; unsupported items are left out; the result is independent of the article), or make conjugation drills from the reviewed pack verbs (pick verbs and tenses; no key needed, and accents always count). One unit is one sheet: numbered rows with typed blanks, inline choices, option picks (gender, odd one out, matching), ordered dialogue lines, word tiles that build a sentence, a reading passage that sits beside its questions, a dictation Listen button and open answers, a check per row and "Check all", hints, explanations of wrong answers, open answers judged by an AI in words (an estimate, never a score), a French character bar, and a unit reset that keeps the history. Words in prompts, reading passages, hints and explanations open the usual word sheet and can be saved to vocabulary, with the current unit’s instructions and passage as context, never an unrelated last-read article. Enter or Space on a word opens lookup without checking an answer; answer fields keep their Enter and composition rules. Answer-choice buttons and word tiles keep their answer actions. A passage opens its exact stored text in the same Reader for listening, word and sentence analysis, pronunciation markers and shadowing, with its own level and the set's name as source. Passage recordings belong to the unit and stay separate from article prose and other units, keep their source and sentence on return from My study or reload, and can be included in backups. Unit reset keeps them; changed or removed passage text keeps playable history without a broken source link; deleting its set removes its passage recordings too. Reading a passage never creates a Library copy. All 11 exercise types are playable (fill in the blanks, choose the option, transform the word, open questions, gender checks, odd one out, matching pairs, ordering a dialogue, building a sentence from word tiles, completing a reading passage, writing numbers from dictation). The open oral assessment (read a sentence of the article aloud and get a pronunciation score) is its own section on the same page.
- **Progress**: three plain figures (words saved, average pronunciation score, shadowing sessions), recent scores, and a tap-to-hear chart of the French sounds in IPA. Grammar accuracy shows real correct/total counts by attempted area, weighted by attempt counts rather than averaging topic percentages, including retained history from deleted sources. One action makes a standalone drill set (no article) targeting up to three weakest nonperfect grammar topics, at the chosen level and size, titled with the drill's own title. The set saves in one step; a failed save retains the generated set for storage-only retry without another AI call, and leaving an unsaved set requires explicit discard. No coaching text or invented scores.
- **Settings**: a page with four sections: AI services, Practice (strict accents, dictation speed, speaking after a check), editable prompts, stored data (saved answers, audio, shadowing recordings) with backup/restore JSON (recordings optional). About is a separate page. AI services is one page with three destinations — Text AI, OCR, Speech synthesis — that each hold a provider and its independently chosen model, one key per provider shared by every destination that names it, and one page-wide Save for all three. The model can be fetched from the provider with the unsaved key and searched, or typed by ID; a fetched list is discovery only, and a model whose support is unconfirmed says so. Each destination can check the key (or preview the voice) without saving; changing a selection never changes another destination's. Practice, prompts and data each save on their own. Language and theme are single icons in the header.

### Grammar learning pilot

The first-chapter pilot is noun gender/feminine forms and noun plurals only. A topic with authored questions shows compact teaching, then three recognition-and-typed pairs, one question at a time. All six questions and the initially closed full reference are freely accessible; checking never advances automatically. Accents always count. Instructions and short verdict/distractor feedback follow the interface language, with longer feedback in a sheet. No AI key, runtime generation/grading, lookup, speech or recording is used in this flow, including saved Practice and Mistakes.

Opening only reads saved exercise state; the first answer saves one revision reused by Grammar and Practice. Redo/reset keep attempts; Mistakes retains the original authored feedback even after deletion. Backups preserve those private snapshots. Learning, feedback and compact-teaching edits create a new revision; unrelated full-reference edits do not. Counts describe current checked/right answers, not mastery or independent recall; skipping/reference use is not tracked. Topics without learning remain reference readers.

Runtime and private authoring/review/publication code are verified with synthetic fixtures only. Real twelve-question language review and local acceptance remain pending; this is not a claim of reviewed or published learning content.

### Vocabulary code versus content

The private explicit-source normalizer groups canonical spellings, records corrections and entry/sense/example omissions, reports quality issues and requires actual hash-bound review before local staged publication. Runtime downloads validate every pinned page and the complete aggregate before one durable offline cache. The service intentionally permits complete-book download; rate limits do not guarantee completion or prevent collection. No real corpus was imported, reviewed, applied or published by this implementation. C1 omission method, target-relevant Canadian usage, corrected artifact/reviewer and distribution approval remain owner decisions. Personal words are never subject to inferred C1 filtering. Paired backups retain learner rows, preferences and all daily ledgers, not disposable book pages or invalidation tokens.

## Tone and personality

A quiet, well-edited reading room: calm, literate, precise. French printed matter is the reference (a good paperback, a dictionary), not a gamified language app. Encouraging in copy but never cute; errors name the problem and the way out.

## Anti-references

Duolingo-style gamification (streak flames, confetti, mascots), generic AI-dashboard styling (gradients, glass, glowing cards, rainbow icons, hero-metric tiles, emoji as icons), and anything that makes the article column feel like one widget among many.

## Design principles

1. **The article is the page.** Reading column 60–70ch, generous leading, a real text face. Everything else is secondary and quieter.
2. **Lookups are detours, not destinations.** Contextual lookups open in a side sheet (bottom sheet on phones) beside the text and never hide it. Centered modals are reserved for tasks that need focus: the first-run guide, import, delete, install. Settings is a page.
3. **One accent, used for action and the current selection.** Colour beyond that is semantic only: success, error.
4. **Show real data or nothing.** No invented scores, streaks or levels; empty states say what to do next.
5. **Touch and keyboard are first-class.** Hover never gates a feature; targets are at least 40px on touch; focus is always visible; motion is short and respects reduced-motion.
6. **Chinese and French typeset together.** Layouts tolerate CJK and Latin mixed lines, long French words and translated strings of different length.

## Constraints

React 19, Vite, Tailwind v4, lucide-react icons, i18next (en/zh), Bun. Fully client-side: AI providers are called from the browser with the user's own keys, and every feature asks one access boundary (`src/services/ai/access.ts`) for its purpose's selection and execution; prompts are editable templates, not code. That boundary is the stated seam where a hosted, paid service could later be supplied — the browser would then reach it instead of the provider — but no account, plan, balance or remote usage exists in this delivery. The one backend is an optional read-only content service for grammar, lexicon and wordbook content (`docs/adr-hosted-content-service.md`): it never sees a key, an answer, an article or a recording (it receives only a topic id, tapped word or book id/page/version), and the app works without it. Client-only persistence: user data in IndexedDB (`src/storage`), settings and API key in localStorage (`relire_*`). Never invent data to fill an empty state.
